Raw capture: raw/pmsg-ramoops-0, 16372 bytes, SHA256

Decoder: 169 records, clean=False, 1 issue (the expected leading wrapped-ring

Physical outcome:
- The user observed only the Apple logo more than six minutes after the
  resume reboot, then returned the device to TWRP. The persistent v68 lock
  screen was NOT restored in this run.
- The ring ends with the same fatal sequence as v69:
  `SBERR libc++abi:`, `SBERR terminating due to uncaught exception of type
  NSException`, `SBERR`. No exception reason, crashing thread or backtrace
  survived. This is a runtime result, not proof that any specific patch caused
  the exception.

Correction result:
- v70 reverted the v69 key/HID diagnostic patches 0447 and 0448. The same
  Apple-logo/NSException outcome remains, so those two diagnostic patches are
  not proven to be the cause and are unlikely to be the sole cause.
- The only remaining source difference from the accepted v68 candidate is 0446,
  a logging-only heartbeat cap. It remains a possible runtime difference but
  is not proven causal. No broad investigation was started.

Framebuffer:
- `SWEET FB: backboardd` selector traffic continued and both SweetHIDTouch and
  SweetHIDButtons user clients opened with `-> 1`.
- Nine `M30 FB: ABI sel=` records survived. Selector 79 appeared twice:
  `in=2/0 s0=0x4d ... out=0/1580` and `in=2/0 s0=0x40 ... out=0/40`.
  This is call-shape evidence only. Selector 79 is still NOT proven to be a
  display-mode structure, and no response was synthesized.
- Heartbeat cap worked: 5 `M30 FB: v=` records survived versus 138 in v68
  and 4 in v69. It reduced pstore churn without changing staged/present
  behavior in the surviving trace.
- Framebuffer staged #1/#2 and present #1/#2 each with copied=10108800,
  followed by done and notify records. The ring then ended in the fatal
  SpringBoard exception. Unlike v69, no third present with copied=0 survived.

Buttons/HID:
- No `A5XELTE KEYS: key=` or `SWEET HID: key` transition records exist in this
  capture. The user confirmed no physical button was pressed after boot, so
  this is an untested acceptance criterion for v70, not a new regression.
- The HID user-client open records show both services were opened by
  IOHIDEventServiceUserClient with one client each. The v69 runtime proof
  (66 GPIO transitions, 67 HID dispatches, 1 client) remains the authoritative
  GPIO-to-HID evidence; v70 did not add or remove functional GPIO/HID code.

Touch:
- No touch contact records, no Melfas hardware driver and no touch support.
  Touch was not tested and must not be claimed.
